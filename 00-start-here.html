Reviewing our incident and mitigation coding

Research draft · Updated October 23, 2026.

We are developing a way to turn AI incident accounts into structured, evidence-linked descriptions, and to connect those descriptions to existing risk frameworks and proposed mitigations. The central question is whether the taxonomy makes coding more consistent, inspectable and useful without claiming more than the sources establish.

The work includes 1,567 AIID development cases, all subsequently included in a 1,597-record full-text exercise. Early findings describe that baseline. A separate 116-entry cohort supports planned cross-model comparison and independent human validation; its model answers and results are withheld here.

Read these short documents in order, or go directly to the part you want to assess:

  1. The risk taxonomy prototype — a self-contained explanation of the five axes, coding rules, worked example, crosswalks and mitigation links.
  2. Where the incidents come from — AIID, OECD, AIAAIC, constructed cases and the meaning of each count.
  3. How we code an incident — independent proposals, reconciliation, challenge, final decisions and human review.
  4. Early findings — what the 1,597-record baseline shows and what it cannot establish.
  5. How we code mitigations — intervention boundaries, risk links, source conditions and open human decisions.
  6. Validation and expansion — the 116-entry study, Sol/Luna/Astra comparison, blinded human coding and staged growth.

Apply the method to your own incidents

The incident coding kit includes the codebook, coding manual, five-stage protocol, input template and launch instructions. Supply your own incident narratives and saved source reports, then use the kit with your chosen AI system or apply the definitions manually.
